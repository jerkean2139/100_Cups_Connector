01

Pulling ideas from a messy meeting

3 STEPS · ~10 MINUTES
10 MIN
+

Most ideation conversations are 90% noise and 10% signal. The job of step one is to pull the signal out — without losing who said what.

Open the raw transcript and show the mess
Before showing what AI can do, we show what it has to work with. Family updates, coffee chat, tangents. Proves the AI is doing real filtering work later — not just summarizing what was already neat.
Run a custom extraction prompt with strict output rules
Generic "summarize this" gives you mush. A purpose-built prompt with required sections — feature list, who pitched what, hard constraints, open questions — gives you something you can actually act on. The prompt is yours to keep.
📋 The Meeting-Mining Prompt
You're an analyst pulling product signal out of a messy meeting transcript. Read the transcript carefully and extract only the parts relevant to building a product. Ignore housekeeping, social pleasantries, off-topic tangents, and personal updates unless they reveal a constraint.

Output your findings in exactly this structure — no preamble, no commentary, no closing remarks:

## 📱 The App Idea
**Working name:** [propose one if not stated]
**Core problem it solves:** [one sentence]
**The "it just works" moment:** [the dream user experience in 1-2 sentences]

## 🧱 Feature List Pulled From the Call
| # | Feature | Who Pitched It | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

## 🚨 Hard Constraints (Don't Miss These)
List every constraint or guardrail the speakers explicitly named. Quote the speaker.

## 🧰 Tech Stack Already Decided in the Call
Bullet list of any platform, framework, language, hosting, or third-party tool the speakers committed to.

## 👥 Stakeholders / Audience
- **Primary user:** [name and role]
- **Secondary users:** [list]
- **Future users / commercial audience:** [if discussed]

## 💰 Business Model Hints
Any pricing, billing, monetization, or commercial signals from the call.

## 🎬 Demo / Storytelling Hooks
Things from the transcript that would make this product memorable to demo.

## ❓ Open Questions
Things the transcript did NOT answer that the user needs to clarify before building.

## ⏭️ Recommended Next Step
Either: Ready for `idea-to-prd` — paste this brief into the skill and answer the 5 questions
Or: Need clarification first — list the 1-3 things to ask the user before scoping

Now extract from this transcript:

[PASTE TRANSCRIPT HERE]
Reveal the structured output and read the names aloud
Credit attribution is the unlock. When you build something from a meeting and credit who pitched each piece, those people become your co-builders, not your audience. They share the win and the story.
Stop scrolling through transcripts looking for the gold. Let the AI find it for you, and have it tell you who said what.
02

Making the plan that prevents scope creep

3 STEPS · ~5 MINUTES
5 MIN
+

This is where most projects die. Vague intent meets unlimited helpfulness and the result is half of three different apps. The fix: a planning interview that pushes back.

Feed the structured brief into a guided planning skill
A skill is just a markdown file with rules — but the rules matter. Five questions max. Mirror back what was said. Force a decision on what NOT to build. The skill is doing the structured thinking so the founder doesn't have to remember to.
Paste this in your Claude Project
Use the idea-to-prd skill. Here's my brief: [paste the structured brief from Step 1 here] Copied!
Watch the scope guard call out feature creep in real time
You'll see it flag things like "you don't need multi-user accounts on day one" or "an email field would do what you're trying to use AI for." That pushback is the whole product. ChatGPT will help you build everything you ask for. This will help you build only what matters.
Save the build plan to your repo as PRD.md
The output isn't a wishlist — it's a plan with a non-negotiable "Phase 2" parking lot for everything cut from MVP. We save it as PRD.md in your GitHub repo so it becomes a permanent artifact you can reference later. Six months from now you'll know exactly what got built and why.

04 · Common Questions

You're going to wonder about these.

Tap any question to expand the answer. We'll cover most of these during the demo too.

Do I need to know how to code? +

No. The whole point of what you're seeing is that you describe what you want in plain English, and Claude writes the code. You'll read code on screen during the demo — but reading is different from writing. You don't need to understand every line. You just need to know what you wanted built and whether it does that thing.

If you can write a clear email describing what you want, you can do this.

What if I don't have a meeting transcript? +

Then start with anything else: a voice memo you talked into your phone, a brain dump you typed into Notes, even a few bullet points. The mining prompt works on any text — meetings are just a common starting point because that's where ideas usually come up.

If you have nothing? Just describe your idea in plain English to the planning skill directly. Skip Part One.

Will I really have a working app at the end? +

Yes — a working version of the core idea, often called an MVP. It won't have every feature you ever dreamed of. The planning step deliberately cuts a lot of features out, because trying to build everything at once is how projects fail.

What you'll have is something real, deployed, and demonstrable. From there, you can ask Claude to add features one at a time.

What's the difference between this and ChatGPT? +

ChatGPT will help you build everything you ask for. Claude Code, especially with a planning skill running on top, will tell you when you're trying to do too much.

That pushback is the difference between "I tried to vibe code and it was a mess" and "I tried to vibe code and I have something working." The skill we're using has a built-in scope guard. It calls things out. ChatGPT, by default, doesn't.

Is this safe? Can the AI break my computer? +

The browser version of Claude Code runs on Anthropic's servers, not yours. It can't touch your computer. Each session runs in an isolated virtual machine that gets destroyed after.

The only thing it touches that's yours is your GitHub repository — and you control what permissions it has there. If you're nervous, start by giving it access to a brand new test repository where there's nothing to break.

What happens after the demo ends? +

Three things. First, you'll have a link to this page — bookmark it. Second, you can download the meeting-mining prompt, the planning skill, and the build prompt right at the bottom. Third, anyone who builds something this week using the kit can send it to me — I'm keeping a list of what people make.

The goal isn't for you to remember what I did. It's for you to do it yourself.

What if my idea isn't a tech product? +

Most of the people I work with aren't tech founders. They have coaching practices, real estate businesses, agencies, podcasts. The kind of apps you'll want to build are usually small, internal tools — a contact tracker, a follow-up system, a content organizer, a custom dashboard.

Big tech-founder-style apps are not the right starting point. Tools that make your existing business 10% smoother are. Start there.

I want the advanced version. Where's that? +

02
Create your GitHub repo
~ 3 min · one new empty repo

This becomes the home for everything your app gets built into. Empty is fine — Claude Code will fill it.

Go to github.com, click + → New repository
Name it something simple like my-first-vibe-app · public or private is your call

An empty repo confuses some tools. README gives Claude Code something to start from.
Click Create repository · note the URL (e.g. github.com/your-name/my-first-vibe-app)
03
Set up your Claude Project with the planning skill loaded
~ 12 min · this is the magic move

Loading the planning skill ONCE into a Claude Project means every chat in that project automatically uses it. You don't have to remember to invoke it. This is the upgrade most people miss.

Go to claude.ai · in the left sidebar click Projects → + New Project
Name it something like My Vibe Coder Workspace · description is optional
Find the Project Knowledge panel · upload SKILL.md from the kit (it's inside idea-to-prd-skill.zip, just unzip and grab it)
SKILL.md is the planning interview rules · 5 questions max, scope guard, mirror back
Also upload BRAND_VOICE.md from the kit · this gives every AI-generated piece of copy your voice instead of generic AI tells
No em-dashes, no lists of three, no "synergy"
Optional but smart: upload a CLAUDE.md file describing your project context (design preferences, tech stack you like, who the audience is). Use the kit's template as a starting point.
CLAUDE.md is your project's "north star" doc · gets referenced by every chat
Find Custom Instructions in the project settings · paste a one-liner like "For any product idea I drop here, follow the workflow in SKILL.md." Now the skill auto-invokes for every chat in this project.
Test it: start a new chat, ask "What skill do you have available?" Claude should describe SKILL.md back. If yes, you're set.
04
Set up auto-accept for Claude Code
~ 2 min · stops the every-five-seconds permission prompts

Claude Code asks for approval before editing files or running bash commands. That's safe but slow. There are two ways to skip the pauses (use whichever fits your situation).

Option A · Use the UI toggle (easiest for beginners) · When Claude Code first asks "Allow this?", look for "Allow always for this session" and click that. Done.
Option B · Drop the kit's settings.json into your repo · Inside your project folder, create a .claude/ folder, then paste the kit's claude-settings.json as settings.json inside it. Sets acceptEdits mode plus a safe allowlist of bash commands.
Path: your-repo/.claude/settings.json · in the kit at the bottom of this page
Why it's safe · The settings file auto-approves common dev commands (npm, git, etc.) but still BLOCKS dangerous things like rm -rf and sudo. You're not turning off the safety, you're skipping the chatter.
